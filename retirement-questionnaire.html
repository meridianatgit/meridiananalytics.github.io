Retirement Forecast
Engine Version 5.0  ·  Questionnaire Version 10  ·  2026-05-11

Thanks for your interest in the retirement forecasting model.

What this model does: This analysis is intended to help people estimate about how long their resources will last in retirement. For those who are still working, it can help you determine when you should retire. For those who have already retired, it can give you some indication of whether your existing resources are sufficient. The analysis is particularly geared toward current and former federal employees, but other clients can use it as well.

How the model works: The model performs what's called a Monte Carlo analysis. That is, it takes the information you input about your income, expenses, savings, and goals, along with estimates of several broad economic variables – like inflation, stock market performance, etc. – and runs repeated simulations. You will also be asked when you retired or, if you are still working, you can choose up to four potential future retirement dates to analyze. In each simulation, the economic variables are allowed to vary, based on an estimate of how they fluctuate in the real world. The simulations are then analyzed to make a probabilistic estimate about your financial security.

Output: The analysis will provide you with three values for each actual or hypothetical retirement date you select (or pair of dates, if you are completing it as a couple):

  • A very conservative estimate for how long your resources will last. That is, in 95% of the simulations your resources will last at least until this age. Put another way, this assumes very bad market conditions.
  • A conservative estimate: In 75% of the simulations your resources will last at least until this age. This assumes bad, but not awful, market conditions.
  • The median estimate: In half the simulations your resources will last this long.

Data needed from you: In the pages that follow, you will be asked for financial information. This includes your salary, savings, spending patterns, children's college dates and expectations, and, finally, retirement plans. To fill this out, you may need:

  • An SF-50
  • Data on your expected Social Security payouts from www.ssa.gov.
  • Information on your TSP holdings.
  • Information on your savings – specifically, what is in stocks, bonds, savings, checking, etc.

Each question includes a short description, as well as a longer description if you click on the adjacent arrow. I strongly encourage you to read all of the longer prompts to make sure you are filling out the form correctly. Some questions may look intuitive, but may not be. Whenever you don't have precise data, it's OK to ballpark.

Spending patterns: The model relies heavily on an estimate of your spending needs in retirement. But most people have only a vague idea of this amount. What I did myself was: over a 12-month period I looked back at my bank statements to see how much money we deposited in our main bank account (after payroll deductions), and then looked at how much we withdrew – to pay credit card balances, checks, withdrawals, etc. This gave me a rough snapshot of our income and expenditures.

Note: The model does not cut off at a certain age. So it may indicate your resources will last into your 140s. I'm afraid that does not mean you will last into your 140s. But it does indicate you can rest easy about your retirement security.

Saving your work: Your progress is saved automatically in your browser, so you can resume where you left off at any time. If you wish to use a different browser or computer, or if you wish to create a portable copy of your work, you can save your progress to a file using the buttons in the header. If you come back to this site from another browser or computer, you can simply upload your saved copy and resume where you left off. Either way your data is secure, because both saves -- the automatic save to your browser files or the manual save to a portable copy -- are both stored only on your own devices, never on any server.

Once you finish: Once you finish the questionnaire, it will download an Excel spreadsheet. Please email that to me at: dan [at] meridiananalytics [dot] org.

Privacy: The model requires information about your savings and spending, which is obviously kind of personal. I will never offer or share this information with anyone else.

Disclaimer: This model emerged out of a long process of working on my own finances while learning VBA coding. I have to be clear, however, that I am not a financial advisor. As such, I can't provide much information about how to plan your future, though I'm happy to talk about it. So the output you receive is provided "as is" and is in no way a guarantee of your actual financial future. Moreover, I can't warrant against any inaccuracies in the program. But I hope it's useful to you, and I appreciate feedback to improve the model.
